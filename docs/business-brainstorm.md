# Business Brainstorm — Custom Tools for Small Businesses

Notes carried over from a brainstorming session (2026-10-29). Style: brief, back-and-forth dialogue.

## The idea
- Build custom software for small businesses to replace Excel, text messages, sticky notes, etc.
- Start local (meet in person, easier to close), go remote later.
- Pricing: $2,000–$5,000 per build, from $149/mo support.
- Stack: frameworks, similar tech stack across clients, AI-assisted build and maintenance.

## Decisions so far
- **Go broad first, narrow later.** Offer to all small businesses, pick a niche after 2–5 cases. Keep the pitch specific ("simple job-tracking and invoicing tools for service businesses") even while the offer is broad.
- **Personal site, not Beavix.** Selling yourself locally; avoids "why $3k when Beavix tools are free?" confusion. Later: Beavix links to the personal site ("Need something custom?"), and the site shows Beavix as portfolio proof.

## Key advice
- Support must mean something concrete (hosting, backups, small changes, response time).
- Main risk: scope creep. Fixed scope, change requests billed separately, 50% upfront.
- Long-term: niche down so builds become reusable (higher margin, easier referrals).

## Finding first clients
- Network first (friends, family, ex-coworkers: "who's drowning in spreadsheets?").
- Walk-ins: ask owners "what's the most annoying admin task in your week?" Listen, don't pitch.
- 1–2 cheap/free builds for testimonial + case study + referrals.
- Partner with accountants/bookkeepers.
- Chamber of Commerce, BNI, meetups.
- Facebook Ads: not recommended as plan B. If tried: pain-specific ad, free 30-min "workflow audit" offer, tight local targeting, $300–500 test budget, stop if cost per booked call > ~$50–100.
- Better plan B: Google Business Profile, cold email/LinkedIn, answering in local FB groups, Upwork.

## Lead finding (user's hard part; to revisit later)
- Sources: Google Maps by industry, Yelp/Nextdoor/local FB groups, job postings for office admin/scheduler, Chamber member directory.
- System: list of 100 businesses, contact 10/day, expect 2–5% to agree to a conversation.
- Dev shortcut: scrape Google Maps for one industry locally.
- Open question: which 2–3 local industries are most common nearby?

## What's needed
- Must have: one-page site, business email on own domain, Google Business Profile, 1–2 demo tools (job tracker, simple CRM, inventory).
- Soon after: contract template (scope, price, 50% upfront, support terms), LLC + business bank account, one-page leave-behind for walk-ins.
- Skip for now: logo, blog, social media, fancy portfolio.

## Site plan (one page, one goal: book a call)
1. **Hero**: "Stop running your business on spreadsheets and sticky notes." / "I build simple custom tools for small businesses, made for how you work." / Button: Book a free 30-min call.
2. **Sound familiar?**: 4–6 pain bullets (Excel only one person understands, customer info scattered across texts/emails/notebooks, manual weekly invoices, copying data into 3 places).
3. **What I build**: 3–4 examples with small screenshots (job/order tracker, simple CRM, scheduling & reminders, invoicing & reports).
4. **How it works**: free call → build in 2–4 weeks with testing along the way → ongoing support and small changes.
5. **About me**: photo, 2–3 sentences, city, "I meet in person."
6. **Pricing** (optional, recommended): "Projects typically $2,000–$5,000. Support from $149/mo."
7. **FAQ**: changes later? do I own it? what if you disappear?
8. **Final CTA**: repeat button + phone + email.

Skip for now: testimonials (until available), blog, multiple pages.

## Pricing on the site
- **Decided: show it, as a range.** Screens out low budgets, builds trust, and the final number is still set on the call.
- Early cheap/free builds are offered privately as "pilot projects," so nothing on the site changes.
